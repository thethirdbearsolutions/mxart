Parallel Fifths Casebook

Every parallel fifth the check flagged in the nine chorales you listened to, drawn beside the repair, with what you said about each. Fourteen spots in all, one of them a parallel octave.

The rule

A fifth is two notes seven semitones apart, such as C up to G, or that distance plus any number of octaves. After the octave it is the interval that blends most: the two notes fuse into something close to one sound.

When two voices are a fifth apart and both then move the same way to another fifth, for that moment they stop being two lines and become one thickened line. Four-part writing counts that as a fault, because the point of four parts is four lines that can each be followed. The octave version is the same idea.

Two things the rule leaves alone: a fifth that follows a long held note, where one phrase has ended and another begins, and instruments that double each other in octaves on purpose.

How to read the pictures

Each picture is two bars. Time runs left to right, pitch runs bottom to top, one row per semitone. The left picture fades the voices that are not involved; the right one shows all four.
